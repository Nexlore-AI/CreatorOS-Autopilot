### 2026-11-07
- **YouTube — Matt Wolfe:** tool-first roundups. Every item is framed as "here is what you can actually use today", never as an abstract capability.
- **YouTube — Wes Roth:** daily same-day news turnaround. Gets a story out while it is still the story; pacing is fast and front-loaded.
- **YouTube — AI Explained:** one topic per video, sceptical reading of lab claims. Always separates "they report" from "independently measured" — worth copying as a habit, not a script.
- **YouTube — Matthew Berman:** model-release walkthroughs where he runs the benchmark himself on camera. The proof is the segment.
- **X — Rowan Cheung (The Rundown AI):** numbered daily lists. Headline is always a concrete noun plus a number, never a vibe.
- **X — Min Choi:** demo-clip reposts with a one-line "here is why this matters" frame. Shows the result in frame one.
### 2026-11-08
- **aiweekly.co — "AI News Today":** ranked, dated daily feed where every item leads with its number and names the primary source separately from the aggregator. The ranking itself is the editorial act — worth copying as a sourcing habit.
- **learnrudi.com — Rudi Daily AI News:** same-day dated roundups in a fixed five-item structure. Proof that a rigid daily shape is an asset, not a limitation, when the audience comes back every day.
- **aiagentsdirectory.com / aiagentstore.ai — AI Agents news briefs:** agent-only daily briefs. Narrow beat, so the enterprise items are specific rather than generic "AI adoption" copy.
- **24/7 Wall St — Omor Ibne Ehsan:** every article opens with a three-bullet "Quick Read". That block is almost exactly a reel's hook plus two beats, and it is built from the earnings call rather than from a press release.
- **Apiiro (security research write-ups):** reel-shaped research — one mechanism, one count of real artefacts, one fix list, and an explicit note on what they did *not* test. The "what we didn't verify" line is the trust move worth stealing.
- **Note on format risk:** Instagram's own creator guidance says it aims to show *less* of reels that are majority static text, and not to recommend accounts whose content prominently features a photorealistic AI-generated person unless labelled. Our reels are animated + narrated and faceless with no synthetic presenter, which keeps us clear of both — but the motion and voiceover are load-bearing, not decoration.
### 2026-11-09
- **YouTube — AI Daily Brief:** daily audio-first briefing, grouped by theme rather than ranked by recency. Reviewers file it in the same "hype filter" tier as AI Explained. Worth copying: it states the claim and the measurement separately in the same breath, so the caveat never sounds like a hedge.
- **Kingy AI:** cross-category — news, demos and category education in one feed, so a single item can be a launch, a test and a teaching moment. Useful counter-example to our rigid four-slot shape: their explainer earns its place by being attached to a thing they just ran.
- **the-decoder.com:** the fastest at turning a new benchmark paper into a gap-between-claim-and-reality headline, and it names the saturated baseline it is comparing against. The habit to steal is leading with the number that went *down*.
- **Note on format risk (YouTube side):** second-hand, unverified reports this week that YouTube removed a batch of large faceless AI channels as slop. Could not confirm from YouTube directly, so treat as caution not fact — but together with Instagram's static-text down-ranking it points the same way: narration and real motion are what separate us from the slop bucket, and the sourcing line is what separates us editorially.
### 2026-11-10
- **tryvolume.app (outlier tracker, not a creator):** measures each post against *its own account's* median reach rather than against other accounts, and only lists a format once it has worked for 3+ accounts. That is the right denominator and the right threshold — it filters out big accounts coasting on subscriber base. The most useful weekly input found so far; worth re-reading every run.
- **TikTok/IG — @mr.hackerloi:** "Settings that developers hide" ran 187x his own median this week. Builds every post on something the viewer already owns and has never opened. The transferable move is the *possessive*: not "a hidden setting", but "the setting on your machine".
- **TikTok/IG — @engineerbeatsai:** 56x on a post about Java dropping `public static void main`. Treats a language changelog as identity news for the people who type it daily. Proof that a spec-level change can out-perform a product launch if you frame it at desk level.
- **TikTok/IG — @cybersecuritygirl:** 171x on "Your iPhone Is Always Listening To You". The frame is an invisible default with a cost, and the payoff is the switch that turns it off. Worth copying as a *structure* only — ours has to be the honest version, where the cost is real and sourced, because the dishonest version is what the slop bucket is full of.
- **TikTok/IG — @maverickgpt:** 156x on "Craziest Use Case of ChatGPT". Shows the output first and explains the prompt second. Same lesson as the tracker's top format: the result goes in second one.
- **Note on hook data quality:** the "highest-converting hook" percentage tables on vendor blogs (Specific Outcome 45%, POV Realism 42%, …) are derived from paid-ad retention, not organic reels, and no study is ever named. Do not plan off them. The own-account-baseline multiples above are the only hook numbers this run treated as real.
### 2026-11-11
- **TikTok — @iamsolexis (18K):** 211x his own median — the week's highest multiple — on a hook that is nothing but a job title and a salary: "Operational Tech Analyst $132,000". No verb, no claim, no promise. Proof that a number welded to an identity needs no sentence around it. The discipline worth copying is the restraint, not the salary bait.
- **Instagram — @techinherstep (181K):** 58x on "The iPhone Setting That Stops THIEVES". The transferable move is grammatical: *the* setting, not *a* setting. The definite article presupposes one correct answer exists and the viewer does not have it. Cheapest hook upgrade available, and it costs nothing in honesty.
- **Instagram — @computergeeks91 (1.6M):** 62x on "HOW TO CHECK IF SOMEONE IS SPYING ON YOUR PHONE?". A question hook that promises a check the viewer can run in the next sixty seconds. Our equivalent is a `cta` whose chips are three things to go look at, which is already the house shape — worth keeping deliberate rather than habitual.
- **Instagram — @_chrisasante (121K):** 77x on "Set up your PS5 Properly". The implied accusation ("you did it wrong") does the work; the word "properly" is the whole hook. Note the risk: that frame only survives if the correction is real, which is why ours has to stay sourced.
- **Volume tracker (tryvolume.app) method update:** re-measured Nov 10 at 299 posts / 154 accounts (174 TikTok, 125 Instagram), up from 284/145 on Nov 8. Instagram carousels are scored on likes-vs-median because IG exposes no view count on carousels — so carousel multiples and reel multiples are **not** the same metric. Worth remembering before comparing the two columns.
- **New Engen Instagram trends (weekly, Mondays):** reports raw, unpolished content currently out-performing high-production posts, and lists an "AI Will Replace You" reel format pairing a self-deprecating joke with licensed audio. Directly counter to our production-value bet; logged as a real tension rather than dismissed, because the opposite pressure (IG down-ranking majority-static-text reels) is what keeps our narration and motion load-bearing.
### 2026-11-12
- **Instagram — @custom_adventurist (290K):** 64x his own median, the single strongest post in this week's tracker sample. Sits in the "show the finished result in second one" bucket — the artefact is on screen before any claim about it is made. Worth copying as a *frame-one* rule, not as a topic.
- **Instagram — @johnnyytech (91K):** 32x on "SOMEBODY NEEDS TO STOP MOTOROLA". The hook is an accusation aimed at a named company, with the reason withheld. Our honest version is naming the lab and the specific decision in the hook — e.g. Google changing one habit — rather than naming a vague "AI" subject.
- **Instagram — @drtechcto (325K):** 25x on a hook that is literally one word: "MICROSOFT". Same restraint lesson as @iamsolexis last week — a proper noun the audience already has feelings about needs no sentence around it. Risky for us because our value is the number, not the brand, but it argues for putting the company name in line one.
- **TikTok — @quicktutorials_official (16K):** 37x on "All right, I'm going to show you how to use the scanner on your iPhone." Notable as a counter-example: a flat, unstyled, almost apologetic opener still hit 37x because the thing being shown was immediately useful. Evidence that hook *craft* is not a substitute for the viewer already wanting the thing.
- **Volume tracker (tryvolume.app) method update:** re-measured Nov 11 at 320 posts / 156 accounts (186 TikTok, 134 Instagram), up from 299/154 on Nov 10. Format leaderboard shifted: "Settings to change on a brand-new device" now tops by median (45x, 4 accounts) while "Show the finished result in second one" leads by breadth (19x, 10 accounts). Breadth is the more trustworthy column — a 45x median over 4 accounts is one or two posts carrying it.
- **aiweekly.co (sourcing habit, re-confirmed):** its Nov 11 feed separated the primary source from the aggregator on every item, which is how today's AWS Emulator story was traced back to allthingsdistributed.com rather than cited from a roundup. Keep using it as an index, never as a source.
### 2026-11-15
- **MathArena (matharena.ai, SRI Lab at ETH Zurich + INSAIT):** not a creator — a sourcing primitive. It prints an *expected cost* column next to every expected-score column, so the leaderboard itself contains the counter-story. Any benchmark table without a cost column should now feel incomplete to us. This is the single most reusable find of the week.
- **Latent Space (latent.space):** publishes the median alongside the mean and states plainly what it did *not* measure ("accuracy wasn't graded"). The median-vs-mean pairing is itself the content — it is how a reader learns which number to carry. Same trust move as Apiiro's "what we didn't verify" line, logged Nov 8.
- **SiliconANGLE case studies (Paul Gillin):** enterprise write-ups built from one named practitioner's conference talk rather than a press release, with the governance rule quoted as a number (an agent runs alone only above mid-to-high-90s accuracy). The transferable shape is: the gate, not the headcount, is the story.
- **byobot.ai "AI Daily Newsstand" / riorundown.substack.com / samonai.substack.com:** three daily aggregators that all separate the primary source link from the item text. Useful as indexes for the 48-hour sweep; none is ever a source. riorundown is the densest of the three on enterprise deployments with hard numbers.
- **the-decoder.com (habit re-confirmed, second time):** headlined today's biggest model story on the cost multiple rather than the ranking. Logged Nov 9 as "leads with the number that went down" — today it led with the number that went *up*. The constant is: never headline the rank.
- **Note on tryvolume.app:** today's page showed only a self-improvement demo set (41x / 27x / 18x), scored against "avg views" rather than a median, with no measurement date and no AI-niche sample. The 299-posts/154-accounts figures logged Nov 10–12 did not reproduce. Treat the earlier method notes as stale until it is re-checked.
### 2026-11-16
- **virlo.ai (hook-type outlier tables):** the first replacement found for tryvolume, and the most useful thing in today's scan. Publishes an outlier rate per hook *type* over 1.7M+ tracked hooks: POV 27.6%, shock 24.4%, stat 19.7%, call-out 17.7%, how-to 16.9%, against a 19.0% all-types average. The uncomfortable read for us: our house hook is a **stat** hook, which lands within a point of the average — the number is not what buys the open. Also splits by platform: it says TikTok rewards POV/first-person while Shorts rewards numbered/listicle framing.
- **Method warning on virlo.ai:** its outlier ratio is **views ÷ followers**, not views vs the account's own median. That is a different denominator from tryvolume's and it flatters small accounts. Vendor-published, no methodology page with a date range found. Treat the ordering as a signal and the absolute percentages as marketing.
- **viral.app (cross-posting study, 150K cross-posted videos):** directly contradicts virlo on POV — reports POV and "me when" openings doing *worse* than average on TikTok. Logged because the disagreement is the finding: hook-type leaderboards are dataset artefacts, so the only safe transfer is the *frame* (second person, one owned object), never the template.
- **tryvolume.app (third strike):** did not reproduce again today — no AI-niche sample, no measurement date. The Nov 10–12 method notes are now formally stale; virlo is the replacement, with the denominator caveat above.
- **the-decoder.com (habit re-confirmed, third time):** headlined today's biggest model story on the *hardware* it avoided rather than on its rank — "never touched an Nvidia GPU" — when the benchmark table would have supported a rank headline. Same constant logged Nov 9 and Nov 15: never headline the rank. Name the thing that is missing.
- **Meituan's own model card (sourcing primitive, not a creator):** marks competitor scores with `*` when cited from public sources and calls its own in-house numbers "indicative only". A vendor labelling its own benchmark run as indicative is the cleanest "they report" artefact seen so far — quote that phrase rather than paraphrasing the caveat.
